ISO 286 fits explained simply

Nominal size, tolerance grade, clearance and interference fit: the key terms around ISO fits – with practical examples.

Whether bearing seat, guide or press fit: for two parts to work together, their tolerances must be matched. The ISO 286 standard provides a uniform system for this. Here are the most important terms – explained simply.

The basics

  • Nominal size: the size given in the drawing, e.g. Ø 50 mm.
  • Tolerance grade (IT): defines the width of the tolerance zone. IT6 is tighter than IT9. The number in “H7” is the tolerance grade.
  • Fundamental deviation: the letter indicates the position of the tolerance zone relative to the nominal size. Capital letters stand for holes (internal features), lower-case letters for shafts (external features).
  • Deviation: the difference from the nominal size upwards (upper deviation) or downwards (lower deviation).

Clearance, transition or interference?

  • Clearance fit: the shaft is always smaller than the hole – the parts can be assembled and moved easily. Example: H7/g6.
  • Transition fit: depending on the actual sizes, there is either clearance or interference. Good for centred connections that can still be assembled. Example: H7/k6.
  • Interference fit (press fit): the shaft is always larger than the hole. The joint holds by pressure and transmits forces. Example: H7/p6.

The hole-basis system

In practice the “hole-basis” system is usually used: the hole is given the fundamental deviation H (lower deviation = 0), and the desired fit is set via the shaft. The advantage: only a few reamers and plug gauges are needed for the holes.

Proven fits from practice

FitTypeTypical application
H7/f7ClearancePlain bearings, rotating shafts
H7/g6Clearance (close)Sliding guides, pistons, spigots
H7/h6Clearance (very close)Sliding fit, collars, interchangeable parts

H7/n6Transition (tight)Bearing bushes, gear rims
H7/p6InterferencePress fits, bushes, permanently joined parts

A worked example

For Ø 50 H7/g6: the hole lies between 50.000 and 50.025 mm, the shaft between 49.975 and 49.991 mm. This gives a minimum clearance of 9 µm and a maximum clearance of 50 µm.
